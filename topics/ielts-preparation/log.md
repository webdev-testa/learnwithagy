### Lesson: n1 — Grammatical person
- Motivation: number alone cannot determine present-tense forms; "I" and "the university" are both singular but occupy different grammatical-person categories.
- Person describes a participant's role in communication: first person is the speaker(s), I/we; second person is the addressee(s), you; third person is a person/thing referred to rather than addressed as I/we/you, such as he/she/it/they or a named noun phrase.
- For these examples, "the university" can be referred to as "it", so it is third-person singular. Avoid implying that every singular reference takes an -s verb or that singular "they" takes third-person-singular verb forms; these details belong in n2.

### Lesson: n2 — Affirmative present-simple lexical verbs
- Motivation: use the subject's person and number from n1 to select the verb form, rather than copying a nearby noun's ending.
- Scope: affirmative present-simple sentences with verbs such as "need" and "support". I/you/we/they take "need"; he/she/it and corresponding ordinary third-person singular noun subjects take "needs". "They" takes "need" even when it refers to one person.
- Examples: I need support; she needs support; they need support. The change in subject determines the change in verb form.
- Most lexical verbs use -s/-es in third-person singular; spelling changes and irregular forms are taught as needed. "Be" has separate forms and remains a later substep within n2. Do not overgeneralize the -s pattern to modal verbs, infinitives, questions or negatives.
- Reference previously verified: https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/present-simple

### Lesson: n2 — Present forms of be
- Motivation: retain the same subject-first process when the verb has a different set of forms.
